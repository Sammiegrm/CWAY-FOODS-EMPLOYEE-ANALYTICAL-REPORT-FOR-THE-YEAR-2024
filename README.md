What if data could talk? Here’s what it said to me :
🔍 “Sales took the crown 👑” — With 102 top performers, it’s officially the best department to be in!
🧠 “Training isn’t just a checkbox.” Despite 55 completions, performance still varied—41 employees still failed. What’s the hidden gap?

🧓 “Age & diversity tell a quiet tale.” Asian employees averaged 48 years—the highest by race. Diversity is more than a number.

💼 “Zone B & C rule the wallet 💸” — They’re the highest paid zones, with 69 employees each. Zone A might want to catch up.

⚖️ “Married life = balanced life?” Yep, they topped the chart for best work-life scores. Coincidence or correlation?

💡 My takeaway? Behind each bar and pie chart is a story of strategy, culture, and opportunity. As analysts, it’s not about data—it’s about decoding the why behind it.

Recommendations
What did I uncover from this dataset? I was able to put through some recommendations to the stakeholders of this project and below are few of them.
👑 Top Performer? Sales.
 With 102 high scorers, Sales leads the way. But the spotlight isn't just for celebration—it’s a benchmark for others.
📉 Zone A falling behind?
 It has the fewest employees. Lower incentives could be to blame. 🧐 Maybe it’s time for better perks.
🎯 Next Level Goals?
 • Turn those who “Fully Meet” into “Exceeds” through rewards & coaching
 • Don’t forget the underdogs—PIP & Needs Improvement staff need tailored support.
⚖️ Work-Life Balance Insights: 
Married folks seem to have it best. But… why are female employees lagging in balance?
 📌 It’s time to uncover the "why" and rewrite that story with thoughtful policy changes.

💬 My take on this whole analysis is that, a dashboard is just a map. The real journey begins when we ask, “What do we change next?”
